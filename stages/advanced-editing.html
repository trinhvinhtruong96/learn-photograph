Masks edit part of a photo

In one sentence

A mask limits an edit to one part of the photo.

Most edits change the whole photo. A mask limits an edit to one area, such as the sky or a face.

Drawn masks are shapes you draw: brush, circle, ellipse, path and gradient. Parametric masks pick pixels by brightness or color, such as everything bright.

Soft edges hide the change. A feather blends the mask edge into the rest of the photo.

Picture it

Think of painter's tape. You tape off the window frame and paint only the wall.

Example

A bright sky over a dark town: add a gradient mask on the sky in the exposure module and lower it by −0.7 EV. The town stays as it was.

In darktable

In a module, open the masks section and pick a drawn shape. Combine several with combine masks. Soften the edge with feathering radius and blurring radius.

Tone equalizer: dodge and burn by brightness

In one sentence

The tone equalizer lifts or lowers parts of the photo by how bright they are.

Dodge and burn means brightening and darkening small areas. The tone equalizer does this by brightness zones.

Lift the darkest zone and the shadows open up. Lower the brightest zone and the sky calms down. Everything else stays the same.

Picture it

Think of a mixing desk with one slider for each brightness level. You turn up the quiet parts without touching the loud ones.

Example

A backlit face is too dark against a bright sky. Hover over the face and scroll up: the face lifts by +1 EV and the sky stays put.

In darktable

The module has the tabs simple, advanced and masking. Hover over the image and scroll the mouse wheel to lift or lower the zone under the cursor.

Try it now

Open a photo with dark shadows. Lift them with the tone equalizer by hovering and scrolling.

Curves shape contrast

In one sentence

A curve maps dark, middle and bright tones to new values.

A curve is a graph. Left is dark, right is bright. Moving a point up brightens those tones, and moving it down darkens them.

An S-shaped curve darkens shadows and brightens highlights, which adds contrast. Lifting the black end gives a faded, matte look.

Picture it

Think of a dimmer for each kind of tone. The curve tells every tone how bright to become.

Example

A flat landscape on a hazy day: a gentle S-curve gives it punch. A portrait for a soft mood: lift the black end a little for matte shadows.

In darktable

Use the rgb curve module for creative curves. Click on the line to add a point, and drag it up or down.

Try it now

Try an S-curve on one photo and a matte curve on another. Compare them with a snapshot.

Grade color with color balance rgb

In one sentence

Color grading gives shadows and highlights their own color.

First set overall color with global vibrance and chroma. The darktable manual suggests beginners start there.

Color grading then gives the shadows and highlights their own tint. Warm highlights with cool shadows is a classic film look.

Picture it

Think of lighting a stage with two colored lamps. One warms the bright parts, the other cools the dark parts.

Example

A street at golden hour: add a little orange to the highlights and a little teal to the shadows. Check that skin still looks natural.

In darktable

Open color balance rgb. Use global vibrance first. Then open the 4 ways tab: shadows lift, highlights gain, power and global offset.

Styles give a set one look

In one sentence

A style saves your edit so a whole set can share one look.

A preset saves the settings of one module. A style saves several modules at once.

Make a style from your best edit. Apply it to a whole set, then fine-tune each photo.

In darktable

Create a style in the lighttable styles module or from the history stack. Apply a style by double-clicking its name.

HDR and panoramas

In one sentence

Merge a bracket into one HDR photo, and use Hugin for panoramas.

HDR merges several exposures of one scene into one photo with detail in both the sky and the shadows. Shoot the bracket in RAW, on a tripod.

Neither darktable nor GIMP can stitch a panorama. The free tool Hugin does that, if you want to try it.

Picture it

Think of taking three notes from one lecture and combining them into one complete page.

Example

A temple doorway with a bright sky outside: three frames at −2, 0 and +2 EV merge into one photo where both look right.

In darktable

Select the bracket in the lighttable. In the selected image[s] module, click create HDR. A new DNG file is added to the library.

From darktable to GIMP and back

In one sentence

Export a 16-bit TIFF from darktable, retouch it in GIMP, then export the result.

Use darktable for overall edits. Use GIMP for pixel work like removing spots. A 16-bit TIFF keeps the most detail between the two programs.

In GIMP, Save keeps an editable .xcf file with layers. Export makes the finished JPEG.

  1. In darktable, export with file format TIFF and bit depth 16 bit, at full size.
  2. In GIMP, open the .tif file. If GIMP asks about the color profile, keep it.
  3. Check Image › Encoding: it should show 16-bit integer.
  4. Duplicate the layer and retouch the copy.
  5. Use File › Export As (Shift+Ctrl+E) and save as JPEG with quality 90. GIMP starts at 85.
Picture it

Think of a car going from the paint shop to the detail shop. Each place does what it is best at.

Example

A portrait edited in darktable still has two blemishes. Export a TIFF, fix them in GIMP, and export the final JPEG.

In GIMP

Save writes .xcf only. Use Export As for JPEG or TIFF.

Try it now

Heal and clone spots away

In one sentence

Heal and Clone copy clean pixels over spots and dust.

Heal copies texture from a clean area and blends it into the spot. Clone copies pixels exactly, which works better on edges and lines.

GIMP has no automatic content-aware remove. You pick the source by hand.

  1. Duplicate the layer first.
  2. Press H for Heal. Use a soft brush a little larger than the spot.
  3. Ctrl+click on clean skin or sky next to the spot, at the same brightness.
  4. Click once on the spot. Take a new source often, and work at 100% zoom.
  5. On an edge, such as a roofline, use Clone (C) with Alignment set to Aligned.
Picture it

Think of patching a hole in a shirt with cloth from inside the hem. The patch matches because it is the same fabric.

Example

A landscape at f/11 shows three dark dots in the sky. They are sensor dust. Heal each one with clean sky next to it.

In GIMP

Use Heal (H) for skin and sky, and Clone (C) for lines. Toggle the copy layer to compare before and after.

Try it now

Remove 5 spots from one photo with Heal. Compare before and after.

Retouch skin and keep the texture

In one sentence

Smooth only the blotches and leave the fine pores alone.

Real skin has pores. If you blur everything, skin looks like plastic.

Wavelet decompose splits the photo into layers by detail size. The finest layer holds the pores. Middle layers hold blotches and uneven tone.

  1. Remove blemishes first with Heal.
  2. Run Filters › Enhance › Wavelet decompose with 5 scales and a layer group.
  3. Leave scales 1 and 2 alone. They hold the skin texture.
  4. On scale 3 or 4, heal or paint softly at 10 to 20% opacity. Avoid eyes, lips, brows and hair.
  5. Lower the group opacity if the effect looks too strong.
Picture it

Think of a song with bass and treble. You turn down a boomy bass note but keep the crisp treble.

Example

A close portrait shows red blotches on one cheek. Smooth them on scale 3, and the pores on scale 1 stay sharp.

In GIMP

Compare with the group visible and hidden. If you can no longer see pores at 100%, you went too far.

Try it now

Retouch one portrait with Wavelet decompose. Check at 100% that pores are still visible.

Dodge and burn in GIMP

In one sentence

Paint light and shadow onto a gray layer to shape a face or a scene.

Dodge means lighten and burn means darken. In GIMP you can do both on a separate layer, so you never touch the photo itself.

Fill a new layer with 50% gray and set its mode to Soft light or Overlay. The gray disappears. Painting white lightens, and painting black darkens.

Picture it

Think of a painter adding highlights and shadows to a portrait. You do the same with a soft brush.

Example

A portrait looks flat. Paint white at low opacity on the cheekbones and black under the jaw. The face gains shape.

In GIMP

For the layer method, use Layer › New Layer, fill it with 50% gray and set Soft light or Overlay. For the tool, press Shift+D for Dodge / Burn and hold Ctrl to switch.

Try it now

Dodge and burn one portrait on a gray layer at 5 to 15% opacity.

Layers, masks and effects

In one sentence

Layers, layer masks and layer effects keep every change editable.

A layer mask hides parts of a layer. White shows the layer and black hides it.

GIMP 3 adds filters as layer effects. They stay editable until you merge them.

Picture it

Think of tracing paper over a drawing. You can change or remove each sheet without harming the drawing below.

Example

You sharpen a whole portrait, but the skin gets too crisp. Add a layer mask and paint black over the skin to hide the sharpening there.

In GIMP

Add a mask with Layer › Mask › Add Layer Masks. Filters you apply show as Layer Effects with an fx icon next to the layer.

Practice

Common mistakes

  • Pushing saturation as far as it goes.

    Compare with a snapshot. Edit for the story, not for color alone.

  • Smoothing skin until it looks like plastic.

    Leave the finest detail scale alone so pores stay.

  • Using masks with hard edges.

    Feather the mask so the change blends in.

  • Editing each photo of a set in a different way.

    Build a style and apply it to the whole set.
