Distance sets the framing

In one sentence

With the 85mm, your distance decides how much of the person fills the frame.

With the camera held vertically, stand about 1.5 m away for head and shoulders. Stand about 2.5 m away for waist-up, and about 5 m for full body.

Held horizontally, the frame is shorter, so step back about 1.5 times as far. At these distances the nose looks natural, not stretched.

Picture it

Think of talking with someone. At a friendly distance their face looks normal. Lean in very close and the nose seems to grow.

Example

A headshot of your sister: stand about 1.5 m away with the camera vertical. Her face fills the frame and looks natural.

On your α7 II

Count your steps: about two steps for head and shoulders, three for waist-up, and six or seven for full body.

Try it now

Photograph one person at 1.5 m, 2.5 m and 5 m with the camera vertical.

Settings for sharp, soft-background portraits

In one sentence

Pick the aperture for the number of faces, and keep the shutter at 1/200 s or faster.

For one person, use f/1.8. For two people at different distances, use f/2.8 to f/4 so both faces are sharp.

Keep the shutter at 1/200 s or faster to freeze small moves. The α7 II cannot set a minimum shutter speed in A mode, so use M with ISO AUTO.

For a still person, use AF-S and hold your Eye AF key. For a person who moves, use AF-C with Flexible Spot or Lock-on AF.

Picture it

Think of the sharp slab from stage 2. Two faces at different distances need a deeper slab, so close the aperture a little.

Example

Window portrait: 85mm, f/2, 1/200 s, ISO 800. The person sits 1 m from the window, turned 45° toward it.

On your α7 II

Turn the mode dial to M and set ISO AUTO. Set the aperture with the front dial and 1/200 s with the rear dial. The camera picks the ISO.

Try it now

Shoot one person at f/1.8 and two people at f/2.8. Check that every face is sharp.

Background distance makes the blur

In one sentence

The farther the background is behind the person, the softer it gets.

Background blur grows with the distance between the person and the background. Move the person away from the wall, and the wall turns soft.

From the same spot, the 85mm blurs the background about 4.7 times more than the 35mm. Framed the same way, it blurs about 1.5 times more. Try both in the simulator below.

Picture it

Think of a sharp slab again. The background is far outside it, and the farther away it is, the softer it looks.

Example

A person stands against a brick wall: the bricks look sharp. Ask them to step 4 m forward, and the bricks turn into soft color.

On your α7 II

Pick the background before you pick the pose. Look for space behind the person: a park, a long street or a far wall.

Try it now

Shoot one person with the background 1 m behind them, then 5 m behind. Keep everything else the same.

Find flattering light

In one sentence

Soft light from the side or front flatters most faces.

Open shade gives soft, even light. A window at 45° gives gentle shape. Backlight with a reflector gives a glowing outline and a lit face.

Golden-hour sun behind the person adds a warm rim. Avoid noon sun from above: it puts dark shadows in the eyes.

Picture it

Think of the hand test from stage 4. Hold your hand where the face will be, and look at the shadows before you shoot.

Example

At noon, move the person under a tree at the edge of the shade, facing the open sky. The light turns soft and the eyes sparkle.

On your α7 II

In backlight, expose for the face. Turn the exposure compensation dial up until the face looks right, and let the sky go bright.

Try it now

Shoot one person in open shade, by a window and backlit with a reflector.

Direct with actions, not poses

In one sentence

Give people something to do, and natural poses follow.

Small changes help most people look good. Turn the body 45° from the camera, put the weight on the back foot, and bring the chin forward and slightly down.

Give the hands a job: hold a cup, touch a collar, put them in pockets. Ask for actions like walking toward you, not for stiff poses.

Talk while you shoot, and show a good frame on the screen. People relax when they see a nice photo of themselves.

Picture it

Think of a film director. They do not say "smile". They say "tell me about your trip", and the smile comes by itself.

Example

Your friend stands stiffly. Ask them to walk toward you and look past you at a bird. Shoot as they walk, and the fifth frame looks natural.

On your α7 II

Use Continuous AF and a burst for moving actions. Pick the best frame later in darktable.

Try it now

Direct a friend through 5 actions: walk, turn, look away, laugh, sit.

Frame without awkward crops

In one sentence

Crop between joints and keep the eyes near the upper third.

Cutting through a knee, an elbow or a wrist looks like a limb was chopped off. Crop between joints instead, such as mid-thigh or mid-upper-arm.

Put the eyes near the upper third line of the frame. Leave a little space above the head, but not too much.

Picture it

Think of a tailor. They cut cloth between the seams, never through them.

Example

A waist-up portrait cuts at the wrists. Step back a little so the frame cuts at mid-thigh instead, and the hands are whole.

On your α7 II

Turn on Grid Line with Rule of 3rds Grid and place the eyes on the upper line.

Try it now

Shoot one portrait cropped at a joint and one between joints. Compare them.

Environmental portraits with the 35mm

In one sentence

Show a person in their own place to tell their story.

An environmental portrait shows the person and where they live or work. The place tells part of the story.

Use the 35mm and step closer than you think. Keep the person large enough, and use the place as the frame.

Picture it

Think of a book cover that shows the hero in their world. You learn who they are before you read a word.

Example

A tailor in her shop: the 35mm at 1.5 m shows her, the sewing machine and rolls of fabric. You see her craft at once.

On your α7 II

Close the aperture to f/4 or f/5.6 so the place stays readable, not blurred away.

Try it now

Make one environmental portrait of someone at work or at home.

Key terms

Working distance
How far you stand from the person.
Environmental portrait
A portrait that shows the person's place.
Reflector
A light surface that bounces light into shadows.
Rim light
Light from behind that outlines the person.
Headroom
Space above the head in the frame.

Practice

Common mistakes

  • Using f/1.8 for two people at different distances.

    Use f/2.8 to f/4 so both faces are sharp.

  • Standing very close with the 35mm for a headshot.

    The nose looks bigger up close. Step back and use the 85mm.

  • Cropping at the knees or wrists.

    Crop between joints.

  • Giving stiff poses.

    Give actions: walk, turn, laugh.

  • Shooting faces in noon sun.

    Move into open shade.

Self-check

How far do you stand for a waist-up portrait with the 85mm held vertically?

About 2.5 m.

Two people stand at different distances. Which aperture do you pick?

f/2.8 to f/4.

How do you get more background blur without changing lenses?

Move the person farther from the background, step closer, or use a smaller f-number.

Why use M with ISO AUTO for portraits on the α7 II?

A mode cannot set a minimum shutter speed. M with ISO AUTO keeps 1/200 s.

Where should a sky mask's edge go?

Along the horizon, with a soft feather.
