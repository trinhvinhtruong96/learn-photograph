Handheld in low light

In one sentence

Open the aperture first, keep a safe shutter speed, and let ISO do the rest.

In low light, start with the widest aperture: f/1.4 on the 35mm. Then pick a shutter speed that keeps people sharp.

Use 1/60 s for a person holding still with the 35mm, 1/125 s for candid moments, and 1/250 s for people walking. For still scenes, IBIS lets you go down to about 1/8 s on the 35mm or 1/20 s on the 85mm.

ISO takes the rest. ISO AUTO stops at 6400 by default, so raise the limit for night work.

Picture it

Think of the bucket in the rain again. At night the rain is light, so you open the bucket wide and wait as long as you safely can.

Example

A dim street, about EV 4: 35mm, f/1.4, 1/125 s, ISO 1600. People walking slowly stay sharp.

On your α7 II

Raise ISO AUTO Maximum above 6400 for night streets. Keep SteadyShot on when you shoot by hand.

Try it now

Shoot 10 frames on a lit street at f/1.4 and 1/125 s. Check them at 100%.

Choose noise over blur

In one sentence

Noise can be cleaned later, but blur cannot be fixed.

At night you trade noise against blur. A higher ISO adds grain, while a slower shutter risks blur.

Choose the grain. You can reduce noise in darktable with denoise (profiled) or neural restore. No tool can make a blurry face sharp.

Picture it

Think of a slightly grainy old film photo next to a smeared one. The grainy one still tells the story.

Example

You shoot a friend at 1/15 s and ISO 800: the face is blurred. At 1/125 s and ISO 6400 the face is sharp, and denoise cleans the grain.

On your α7 II

When in doubt, raise ISO by one stop and use the faster shutter speed.

Try it now

Shoot one scene at 1/15 s and ISO 800, then at 1/125 s and ISO 6400. Compare at 100% after denoise.

Focus in the dark

In one sentence

Aim autofocus at a contrasty edge, or focus by hand with magnification.

Autofocus needs contrast. In the dark, aim at a bright edge such as a lit sign or a window frame.

If it still hunts, switch to manual focus. Magnify the view and turn the ring until the edge is crisp.

Picture it

Think of reading in a dark room. You find the brightest page edge first, then the words.

Example

A dark alley: autofocus hunts on the wall. You focus on the lit café sign at the same distance, then recompose.

On your α7 II

Switch to Manual Focus and use Focus Magnifier or MF Assist to zoom in. Peaking Level helps on bright edges.

Blue hour, light trails and reflections

In one sentence

Blue hour, car lights and wet streets make the city glow.

Blue hour gives a deep blue sky while city lights are on. It is often better than full night, because the sky still has color.

For light trails, put the camera on a tripod. Use ISO 100, f/8 to f/11 and 5 to 30 s. Longer exposures make longer trails.

If the background is too bright, close to f/11 or f/16. After rain, wet streets reflect the lights.

Picture it

Think of drawing with a sparkler in the dark. The camera sees every place the light has been.

Example

A bridge at blue hour: tripod, ISO 100, f/11, 15 s. Car lights become red and white lines and the sky stays blue.

On your α7 II

Exposures of 1 s or longer start noise reduction that takes as long again. Wait before the next frame.

Try it now

At blue hour, shoot traffic from a tripod at ISO 100, f/8, for 10 s.

Mixed light and white balance

In one sentence

Street lights come in different colors, so shoot RAW and fix it later.

Old sodium street lamps glow orange, at about 2000 to 2200 K. White LED street lights are about 3000 to 4000 K.

One street can have both. No single white balance fits every lamp, so shoot RAW and adjust in darktable.

Picture it

Think of a room lit by candles and a phone screen. Each light paints its own color.

Example

An old market street: orange lamps on one side and white shop lights on the other. You set white balance for the faces under the white lights.

On your α7 II

Set White Balance to Auto as a start. Fix it later with color calibration in darktable.

Try it now

Find a street with two kinds of lights and photograph a person under each.

Your first star photo

In one sentence

Go dark, focus on a bright star, and keep the exposure short enough for sharp stars.

Find a dark place away from city light, on a night without a moon. Check a light-pollution map first.

Focus on a bright star with manual focus and magnification. Then keep the exposure short, because the stars move across the sky.

The NPF rule gives the longest time before stars smear. For the 35mm at f/1.4 it is about 6.5 s, and at f/2 about 7.1 s. The older 500 rule says 14 s, which leaves short trails.

Use ISO 3200 to 6400. Corner stars may look like small comets, called coma. Test f/2 and f/2.8 on your lens.

Expect a dark file and brighten it in darktable. Stacking many frames with Sequator (Windows) or Siril (free) cuts noise.

The Milky Way core shows from about February to October in the northern hemisphere. In Vietnam it rises high, about 40 to 50° up. February to April before dawn is best, before the rainy season of about May to October.

Picture it

Think of a slow merry-go-round. A short glance shows a sharp horse, and a long stare blurs it. The stars turn the same way.

Example

A dark beach in March before dawn: 35mm, f/2, 7 s, ISO 6400. The Milky Way core shows above the sea, and the stars are dots.

On your α7 II

Set Manual Focus, magnify a bright star and turn the ring until it is smallest. Use the 2-second self-timer on a tripod with SteadyShot off.

Try it now

On the next clear, moonless night, shoot the stars with the 35mm at the NPF time.

Stay safe and comfortable

In one sentence

Plan your night shoot so you stay safe.

Go with a friend and tell someone where you are going. Carry a headlamp with a red light, so your eyes stay used to the dark.

Bring water, a warm layer and spare batteries. The NP-FW50 battery lasts about 270 shots with the viewfinder.

Picture it

Think of night photography like a short hike. You plan the route and pack before you go.

Example

Before an astro trip, you check the weather, share your location with a friend and pack two spare batteries.

On your α7 II

Pack the camera, both lenses, a tripod, spare batteries and a lens cloth for dew.

Key terms

NPF rule
A formula for the longest star exposure without trails.
500 rule
An older, looser rule: 500 divided by focal length.
Coma
Corner stars that look like small comets.
Light trails
Streaks from moving lights in a long exposure.
Light pollution
Sky glow from city lights that hides stars.
Stacking
Merging many frames to cut noise.

Practice

Common mistakes

  • Keeping ISO low and getting blur.

    Raise ISO. Noise can be cleaned, blur cannot.

  • Using the 500 rule for stars.

    Use the NPF rule. It gives shorter, sharper times.

  • Trusting autofocus in the dark.

    Switch to manual focus and magnify a bright light or star.

  • Using one white balance for mixed lamps.

    Shoot RAW and adjust in darktable.

  • Leaving with one battery.

    Carry 2 spares.
