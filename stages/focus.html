Only one distance is truly sharp

In one sentence

Your lens focuses at one distance, and sharpness fades in front of and behind it.

When you focus, one distance becomes truly sharp. Things a bit closer or farther still look sharp enough. That zone is the depth of field.

Depth of field gets thinner when you open the aperture. It also gets thinner when the subject is bigger in the frame. Stepping closer or using a longer lens both make the subject bigger.

At portrait distances the zone splits about evenly. Roughly half lies in front of the face and half behind it.

Picture it

Think of a slab of glass standing in front of you. Everything inside the slab looks sharp. The edges of the slab are soft, not hard.

Example

A portrait with the 85mm at f/1.8 from 2 m: the sharp slab is only about 6 cm deep. The eyes are sharp, but the ears can already look soft.

On your α7 II

Use the A mode from stage 1. A smaller f-number makes the slab thinner. A bigger one makes it deeper.

Try it now

Focus on a cup 1 m away with the 35mm. Shoot at f/1.4 and at f/8. Compare how much of the table is sharp.

Focus Mode: still or moving

In one sentence

Pick a focus mode for still subjects or for moving ones.

The camera has four focus modes. Single-shot AF (AF-S) focuses once and locks. Use it for still subjects.

Continuous AF (AF-C) keeps focusing while you half-press. Use it for subjects that move. DMF lets you fine-tune by hand after AF, and Manual Focus is fully by hand.

Picture it

AF-S is like taking aim once at a still target. AF-C is like following a moving target with your eyes.

Example

A friend sits in a café: use AF-S. A child runs across a park: use AF-C.

On your α7 II

Go to MENU › Camera Settings › Focus Mode and pick Single-shot AF or Continuous AF.

Try it now

Set AF-C and follow a moving car or bike with a half-press. Watch the focus point stay on it.

Focus Area: where the camera looks

In one sentence

Focus Area tells the camera which part of the frame to focus on.

Wide lets the camera choose. It often picks the closest or brightest thing, which may be the background.

Zone uses a block of the frame. Center uses the middle. Flexible Spot uses one small spot that you move, in sizes S, M and L.

Lock-on AF follows a subject as it moves around the frame. Start with Flexible Spot M: you decide what is sharp.

Picture it

Wide is like asking a friend to take the photo for you. Flexible Spot is like pointing at the exact thing you want.

Example

A person stands in front of a busy shop window. Wide focuses on the window. Flexible Spot M on the eye gets the person sharp.

On your α7 II

Go to MENU › Camera Settings › Focus Area and pick Flexible Spot M. To move the spot quickly, assign Focus Standard to a key. This needs firmware 4.00 or later.

Try it now

Set Flexible Spot M. Move the spot onto different objects and take one photo of each.

Eye AF puts focus on the eye

In one sentence

Eye AF finds an eye and focuses on it for you.

In a portrait, the eye closest to you must be sharp. That is where people look first.

At wide apertures the sharp slab is thin. With the 85mm at f/1.8 from 2 m, only about 5.7 cm is sharp. Focus on the nose and the eyes go soft.

Picture it

Back to the slab of glass. At f/1.8 it is as thin as a book. You want it to pass right through the nearest eye.

Example

Your friend turns a little to the left. Hold the Eye AF key: the camera picks an eye by itself. Check that it boxed the nearer eye, and if not, put Flexible Spot S on that eye.

On your α7 II

Assign it with MENU › Custom Settings › Custom Key Settings. Pick a key and choose Eye AF. Hold that key while you press the shutter.

Use it in AF-S. Sony warns that Eye AF may not work in AF-C.

Try it now

Assign Eye AF to a key. Shoot a friend at f/1.8 and check the nearest eye at 100%.

Back-button focus separates focus from the shutter

In one sentence

With back-button focus, a rear button focuses and the shutter button only takes the photo.

Normally a half-press focuses. With back-button focus, you press a button with your thumb to focus instead.

Focus once, let go, and the focus stays put. Then shoot as many frames as you like without the camera refocusing.

Picture it

It is like splitting one job between two hands. Your thumb aims, and your finger takes the photo.

Example

A street musician plays in one spot. Focus on them with your thumb once. Then shoot ten frames as they move their hands, with no refocusing.

On your α7 II

Set MENU › Custom Settings › AF w/ shutter to Off. Then go to Custom Key Settings › AEL Button and choose AF On. Keep the switch lever on AEL.

Try it now

Set up back-button focus. Focus on a still object with your thumb, then take 5 photos without pressing it again.

Manual focus with peaking

In one sentence

Peaking colors the sharp edges, so manual focus becomes easy.

Sometimes autofocus struggles, for example in the dark. Then you turn the focus ring by hand.

Peaking draws colored outlines on edges that are in focus. It only works in Manual Focus and DMF. It is Off by default, so turn it on.

Picture it

Peaking is like a highlighter for sharp edges. Turn the ring until the colored lines sit on your subject.

Example

At night, autofocus hunts on a dark building. Switch to Manual Focus and turn the ring until the window edges glow red.

On your α7 II

Set MENU › Custom Settings › Peaking Level to Mid. Pick Peaking Color: Red, Yellow or White. Set MF Assist to On to zoom in when you turn the ring.

Try it now

Turn on peaking. Focus by hand on 5 objects and check them at 100%.

What makes photos blurry

In one sentence

Most blur comes from a slow shutter or missed focus, not from the lens.

  • A slow shutter: your hands shake during the photo.
  • Subject motion: the person moves during the photo.
  • Missed focus: the camera focused on the wrong thing.
  • Focus and recompose at f/1.4: turning the camera moves the sharp slab off the eye.
  • Very small apertures, around f/16: diffraction softens fine detail.
  • A dirty lens: smudges make the photo hazy.
Picture it

Think of a laser pointer again. Turn it a little and the dot moves a lot. Turning the camera moves the sharp slab the same way.

Example

You focus on an eye at f/1.4, then turn the camera to put the face on the left. The eye now sits in front of the sharp slab and looks soft.

On your α7 II

For lenses without electronic contacts, set SteadyShot Adjust. to Manual and enter SteadyS. Focal Len.. If very fast shutter speeds wide open make blur edges look cut off, set e-Front Curtain Shut. to Off.

Try it now

Find your handheld limit: shoot a still object from 1/60 s down to 1/4 s with each lens. Check every frame at 100%.

Key terms

Focal plane
The one distance that is truly sharp.
Depth of field
The zone in front and behind that looks sharp.
Bokeh
The look of the blurred parts.
AF-S
Focuses once and locks, for still subjects.
AF-C
Keeps focusing while you half-press, for moving subjects.
Focus area
The part of the frame the camera uses to focus.
Peaking
Colored outlines that show sharp edges in manual focus.
Diffraction
Softness from very small apertures, around f/16.
Hyperfocal distance
The focus distance that keeps infinity acceptably sharp.

Practice

Common mistakes

  • Focusing on the nose or the ears.

    Put the focus point on the nearest eye.

  • Leaving Focus Area on Wide.

    Wide may pick the background. Use Flexible Spot M.

  • Believing the sharp zone is one third in front and two thirds behind.

    That is only true far away. At portrait distance it is about half and half.

  • Thinking the smallest aperture is the sharpest.

    Very small apertures, around f/16, soften detail.

  • Blaming the lens for blur.

    Blur is usually a slow shutter or missed focus.

Self-check

A friend walks toward you. Which focus mode do you use?

Continuous AF (AF-C).

At 85mm, f/1.8 and 2 m, how deep is the sharp zone?

About 6 cm. Focus on the nearest eye.

You focus on an eye at f/1.4 and turn the camera to recompose. What can go wrong?

The sharp plane swings away, so the eye can go soft. Move the focus spot instead.

What does IBIS not fix?

A subject that moves. It only cancels your hand shake.

Your photo is a bit dark in A mode. What is the fastest fix?

Turn the exposure compensation dial up.
