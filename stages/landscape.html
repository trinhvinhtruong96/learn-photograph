Plan for light and weather

In one sentence

Most great landscapes are planned before you leave home.

Check the weather and the sunrise or sunset time the day before. Look at the place on a map to see where the sun will be.

Arrive 30 minutes early. The best light often starts before sunset and ends soon after.

Picture it

Think of catching a train. If you arrive when it leaves, you miss it. Light leaves on time too.

Example

You want a lake at sunrise. SunCalc.org shows the sun rising behind the hills to the east. You stand on the west shore and arrive in the dark.

On your α7 II

Pack spare batteries and check the memory card the night before. Cold mornings and long waits drain batteries.

Try it now

Plan one sunrise or sunset shoot with SunCalc.org. Write the time and the spot you will stand.

Tripod settings for sharp landscapes

In one sentence

A tripod, a timer and middle apertures give sharp landscapes.

Put the camera on a tripod and use f/8 to f/11, ISO 100 and RAW. This keeps the photo sharp and clean.

Use the 2-second self-timer so your finger does not shake the camera. Sony advises turning SteadyShot off on a tripod.

Exposures of 1 s or longer start noise reduction that takes as long again. Wait before you move the camera.

Picture it

Think of a tripod as a third hand that never shakes.

Example

A waterfall at dusk: tripod, f/11, ISO 100, 2-second timer, SteadyShot off. The rocks stay sharp at 1 s.

On your α7 II

Set Drive Mode › Self-timer: 2 Sec. Turn off stabilization with MENU › Camera Settings › SteadyShot › Off. Turn it back on when you shoot by hand.

Try it now

Set up your tripod at home. Shoot one photo with the 2-second timer and SteadyShot off.

Front-to-back sharpness

In one sentence

Focus at the hyperfocal distance to keep both the foreground and the horizon sharp.

A rock or flower close to the camera leads the eye into the photo. That is foreground interest.

To keep near and far sharp, focus at the hyperfocal distance. With the 35mm at f/8, focus at about 5.1 m, and everything from about 2.6 m to infinity is acceptably sharp.

If the far detail matters most, focus a little farther than that.

Picture it

Think of the sharp slab from stage 2 stretched out like a long carpet. Focusing at the hyperfocal distance unrolls it from your feet to the horizon.

Example

A beach with shells near your feet and an island far away: 35mm, f/8, focus at 5 m. The shells and the island both look sharp.

On your α7 II

Switch to Manual Focus, magnify the view, and focus on something about 5 m away. Then do not touch the ring.

Try it now

Find a scene with something close and something far. Focus at about 5 m with f/8 and check both at 100%.

Horizon, texture and weather

In one sentence

Place the horizon on purpose and welcome dramatic weather.

Put the horizon low to show a big sky, or high to show more land. Keep it level.

Side light shows texture in sand, rock and fields. Fog, storms and rain clouds make moody photos, so do not stay home when the weather turns.

Picture it

Think of the sky and land as two parts of a story. Give more room to the part that has more to say.

Example

Rice fields after rain with storm clouds above: put the horizon on the lower third to show the clouds.

On your α7 II

Turn on Grid Line with Rule of 3rds Grid to keep the horizon level and placed.

Try it now

Shoot one landscape with a low horizon and one with a high horizon. Pick which one tells the story.

Long exposures smooth motion

In one sentence

A long shutter speed turns moving water and clouds smooth.

An ND filter is dark glass that cuts light. ND8 cuts 3 stops, ND64 cuts 6 and ND1000 cuts 10.

With an ND1000, a 1/125 s exposure becomes about 8 s. Silky water starts at about 1/2 s, gets smoother at 1 to 2 s and very smooth at 5 to 10 s.

Picture it

Think of sunglasses for your lens. The darker the glass, the longer the shutter stays open.

Example

Waves at noon: without a filter, 1/125 s freezes them. With ND1000, 8 s turns the sea into soft mist.

On your α7 II

Focus and compose before you screw on a strong ND filter. The view gets very dark through it.

Try it now

Shoot moving water or clouds for 1 s or longer on a tripod, at blue hour or with an ND filter.

Bracket for high contrast

In one sentence

Shoot several exposures and merge them when the scene is too bright and dark at once.

Bracketing means shooting the same scene at different exposures. One frame keeps the sky, another keeps the shadows.

Merge them later with create HDR in darktable, as in stage 6.

Picture it

Think of taking two notes in a loud room: one close to the speaker, one far away. Together they cover everything.

Example

A sunrise over a dark valley: 3 frames at −2, 0 and +2 EV on a tripod. The merged photo shows a colorful sky and a detailed valley.

On your α7 II

Open Drive Mode and choose Cont. Bracket. Pick 3 or 5 frames with steps up to 3.0 EV.

For example, use 1.0EV 5 Image or 2.0EV 3 Image. Hold the shutter until all frames are done.

Try it now

Shoot one 3-frame bracket of a bright window from inside your room.

Tell a travel story

In one sentence

A travel story shows a place through people, details and moments.

Wide views alone feel like postcards. Add people for scale, details like food and signs, transport, and the place at night.

Respect local culture. Ask before you photograph people in temples or homes.

Picture it

Think of telling a friend about a trip. You do not only describe the view. You talk about the food, the people and the bus ride.

Example

A trip to Đà Lạt: a misty hillside, a flower seller, coffee beans in a hand, the bus station, and the night market.

On your α7 II

Carry the 35mm for most travel photos. Switch to the 85mm for portraits and distant details.

Try it now

Make a 6-frame story about a place you visit this week.

Key terms

ND filter
A dark filter that cuts light so you can use slower shutter speeds.
Bracketing
Shooting several frames at different exposures.
Long exposure
A shutter speed long enough to blur motion.
Foreground interest
Something close to the camera that leads into the scene.
Hyperfocal distance
The focus distance that keeps infinity acceptably sharp.

Find the hyperfocal distance

Pick a lens and an aperture. The mark shows where to focus so the far horizon stays acceptably sharp.

Warm-up

Zone-focus a street scene at f/8 and 3 m. Check at 100% what is sharp.

Practice

Common mistakes

  • Leaving SteadyShot on a tripod.

    Turn it Off, as Sony advises.

  • Pressing the shutter by hand on a tripod.

    Use the 2-second self-timer.

  • Focusing on the far horizon.

    Focus at the hyperfocal distance.

  • Putting the horizon in the middle by habit.

    Place it on purpose: low for sky, high for land.

  • Arriving when the sun sets.

    Arrive 30 minutes early. The best light starts before sunset.

Self-check

With the 35mm at f/8, where do you focus for front-to-back sharpness?

About 5.1 m. Everything from about 2.6 m to infinity is acceptably sharp.

You add an ND1000 to a 1/125 s exposure. What is the new shutter speed?

About 8 s.

Why turn SteadyShot off on a tripod?

Sony recommends it, because it can cause blur on a tripod.

Which bracket setting covers a very bright sky?

Cont. Bracket at 2.0EV 3 Image or 1.0EV 5 Image.

Which way does a higher camera WB number move the color?

Warmer.
