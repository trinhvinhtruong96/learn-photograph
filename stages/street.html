Why the 35mm works on the street

In one sentence

The 35mm shows people and their place, and puts you close to the action.

The 35mm sees 54° across. That is wide enough to show a person and the street around them.

To fill the frame, you have to step close. That makes the photo feel like you were there, not watching from afar.

Picture it

Think of standing in the crowd instead of watching it from a balcony. The 35mm puts the viewer in the crowd.

Example

A noodle seller at a stall: from 2 m with the 35mm, you see her hands, the steam, and customers waiting behind.

On your α7 II

Keep the 35mm on the camera for a whole walk. You learn to see its frame without lifting the camera.

Try it now

Walk for 30 minutes with only the 35mm. Take 20 photos from 3 m or closer.

Set up for zone focus

In one sentence

Preset the focus and settings so you can shoot the moment without waiting for the camera.

Zone focus means you set the focus to a fixed distance before you shoot. With the 35mm at f/8 focused at 3 m, everything from about 1.9 m to 7.2 m is sharp.

Use M with ISO AUTO: f/8 and 1/500 s, with the ISO AUTO maximum at 6400. The exposure dial still adjusts brightness.

The other way is A mode with AF-C and the Wide focus area. The α7 II has no silent shutter, so be open, not sneaky.

Picture it

Think of a goalkeeper who stands in the right spot before the kick. You set the sharp zone first, then wait for someone to step into it.

Example

Market at golden hour: 35mm, M mode, f/8, 1/500 s, ISO AUTO, zone focus at 3 m. The camera picks about ISO 800 to 1600. Find a shaft of light and wait for someone to walk into it.

On your α7 II

Set Focus Mode to Manual Focus and turn the ring to 3 m. Set ISO AUTO Maximum to 6400. Turn the mode dial to M and choose ISO AUTO.

Try it now

Set up zone focus at 3 m and f/8. Shoot 20 people walking past without touching the focus.

See light, layers and gestures

In one sentence

Good street photos come from light, layers and a moment that means something.

Look for strong light and shadow first. Then wait for a person to step into it.

Layers put people near, in the middle and far. A gesture, such as a hand raised or a glance, makes a moment. Juxtaposition puts two things side by side so they say something new.

Fishing means picking a good spot and waiting. Hunting means walking and reacting. Try both.

Picture it

Think of a theater. First you find the stage, which is the light. Then you wait for the actor to walk on.

Example

A shaft of late sun across a street corner: wait 10 minutes until a cyclist rides through it. The rest of the street stays dark.

On your α7 II

Pre-set exposure for the bright patch, not the whole street. Use the dial to go darker until the light patch looks right.

Try it now

Find one patch of strong light. Wait there for 15 minutes and photograph everyone who steps into it.

Get close with respect

In one sentence

Be open, be kind, and follow the law where you shoot.

Confidence helps: walk calmly, smile, and hold the camera where people can see it. If someone asks you to delete a photo, delete it.

Take extra care with children and with people in hard situations. Ask yourself if the photo respects them.

Laws differ by country, so check the rules where you shoot. In Vietnam, for example, Civil Code 2015 Article 32 says you need consent to use a person's image. This is not legal advice.

There are exceptions for public events and the public interest. Using a photo to make money needs the person's consent and payment. Under Article 25, a child's consent comes from a parent or guardian.

Picture it

Think of being a guest in someone's neighborhood. A smile and a nod open more doors than a hidden camera.

Example

A street vendor sees you take a photo. You smile, show her the screen, and offer to send it. She laughs and lets you take two more.

On your α7 II

Carry your phone with a way to share a photo. Sending someone their portrait builds trust fast.

Try it now

Ask 3 strangers if you may take their portrait. Thank each person, whatever they say.

Tell a story in 8 photos

In one sentence

A photo essay uses a few different kinds of photos to tell one story.

A photo essay is a short series about one place or event. Each photo has a job.

Start with an establishing shot: a wide view of the place. Add a medium shot, a detail, a portrait, a moment, and a closing photo. Fill the set to 8 with more moments or details.

Picture it

Think of the scenes in a short film. A wide opening shot, closer scenes, and a last shot that ends the story.

Example

A morning market: the wide street at dawn, one stall, hands weighing fish, the seller's face, a child laughing, and the empty street at noon.

On your α7 II

Shoot more than you need. Pick the 8 photos later in darktable, in the order the story happens.

Try it now

Plan an 8-photo essay about a place near you. Write the 6 photo jobs on a card first.

Work a scene and build a series

In one sentence

Stay with a scene, come back to it, and follow one theme.

The best street photos often come on the third or fourth visit to the same place. You learn where the light falls and when people pass.

A theme ties a series together: hands, shadows, red, or people waiting. Pick one theme for a whole walk.

Picture it

Think of a fisherman who returns to a good spot every morning. He learns when the fish come.

Example

A bus stop near your home: you visit it at 7 am for a week. On day 5, a man in a red coat stands under the only sunbeam.

On your α7 II

Note the time and light of each visit. Return when the light is best.

Try it now

Pick one spot and visit it 3 times this week at the same time.

Practice

Common mistakes

  • Hiding and shooting from far away.

    Step closer and stay open and friendly.

  • Using AF Wide in a busy crowd.

    Wide may pick the wrong person. Zone focus, or use Flexible Spot.

  • Using 1/60 s for people walking.

    Use 1/500 s.

  • Publishing or selling a stranger's portrait without asking.

    Ask for consent to use it, and check your local law.

Self-check

With the 35mm at f/8 focused at 3 m, what is sharp?

About 1.9 m to 7.2 m.

Why use M with ISO AUTO on the street?

You fix f/8 and 1/500 s and the camera picks the ISO. A mode cannot set a minimum shutter speed.

Someone asks you to delete their photo. What do you do?

Delete it and thank them.

Name the six parts of a photo essay.

Establishing, medium, detail, portrait, moment, closing.

What makes a background look larger next to a person?

Standing farther back with a longer lens.
